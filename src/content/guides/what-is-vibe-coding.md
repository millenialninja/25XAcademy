## Where the name comes from

The term was popularised in early 2025 by AI researcher Andrej Karpathy, who described a new way of programming where you "fully give in to the vibes": you tell the AI what you want, accept what it produces, and keep steering it with more instructions.

Since then, AI builders such as **Lovable**, **Bolt** and **Replit** have made this practical for people who have never written code. You type what you want in plain English. The builder writes the code, shows you a working preview, and lets you keep changing it by chatting.

## How it works, step by step

1. **Describe the tool.** For example: "Build a 6-question GST readiness check for small business owners that scores them out of 100."
2. **The AI builds a first version.** In a minute or two, you see a working screen in your browser.
3. **You test it like a customer would.** What's confusing? What's missing?
4. **You refine it in plain language.** "Make the questions shorter. Ask for name and WhatsApp number before the full result."
5. **You publish it.** The builder gives you a link you can share on WhatsApp, LinkedIn or as a QR code.

The skill isn't coding. It's **describing clearly what you want**, and judging whether the result is right for your customer. That's why business owners often do well at it: they know their customers better than any developer does.

## What a business owner can realistically build

| Works well | Needs more care | Not a fit (yet) |
|---|---|---|
| Lead tools: scorecards, readiness checks, quizzes | Tools that store customer data (you need security checks) | Banking-grade or medical systems |
| Calculators and estimators | Payments and subscriptions | Apps that must be in the App Store |
| Booking and pre-check forms | Logins with different user roles | Very large, complex platforms |
| Internal tools: trackers, dashboards, checklists | Connecting to other software (APIs) | |
| Simple products you sell to others | | |

## The limits, honestly

- **AI makes mistakes.** It can produce something that looks right but behaves wrongly. You must test it, especially anything with logins or customer data.
- **Security is your job.** If your tool stores leads, check that one user can't see another's data. In our programs we teach a simple **two-user safety test** for exactly this.
- **The law still applies.** If you collect personal data in India, the [DPDP Act](/guides/collect-leads-legally-india-dpdp) applies to you.
- **Clear instructions matter.** Vague prompts give vague tools. A simple structure like the [RAO method](/rao-method) makes a big difference.

## Vibe coding vs hiring a developer vs no-code builders

- **Hiring a developer or agency:** best for large or complex systems. Slower and more expensive for small tools, and every change goes back through them.
- **Older no-code builders** (drag-and-drop): good for websites and forms, but you're limited to what the builder's blocks allow.
- **Vibe coding with an AI builder:** fastest for small, custom tools, and you can change anything by describing it. You still need to test carefully.

## How to start

1. Pick **one question your customers always ask** before they buy. That's your first tool.
2. Write it as a one-page brief: who it's for, what it asks, what result it gives, and what you want to receive.
3. Turn the brief into a prompt with the [RAO method](/rao-method).
4. Build it in an AI builder, test it with two people, then share it.
