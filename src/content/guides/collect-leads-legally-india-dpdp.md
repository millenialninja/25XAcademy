> This guide is general information, not legal advice. For your specific situation, speak to a lawyer.

## The law in one paragraph

India's **Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023** (the DPDP Act) sets the rules for using personal data in digital form. The **DPDP Rules, 2025** were notified in November 2025 and phase in over 18 months: most day-to-day duties, such as notices, security and responding to people's requests, apply from around **May 2027**. Until then, the older IT Act rules on privacy policies still apply. The simplest approach is to build your tool the DPDP way from the start.

## Are you covered?

If your business tool, website or WhatsApp list collects information that identifies a person (name, phone number, email, and so on), **yes**. You decide why and how the data is used, so you are the **Data Fiduciary**. The AI builder or host you use (for example Lovable or Cloudflare) is your **Data Processor**: they handle data on your behalf.

## The checklist for a lead tool

1. **Collect only what you need.** For most lead tools: name, WhatsApp number, and the answers. Don't ask for date of birth, income or health details unless the tool truly needs them.
2. **Show a short notice before they submit.** In plain language: what you collect, why, and how to contact you. Link to your privacy policy.
3. **Ask for consent with a clear action.** An unticked checkbox or a clear "Yes, contact me" button. Never a pre-ticked box.
4. **Use it only for that purpose.** If someone asked for their GST score, you can follow up about GST. Adding them to unrelated promotions needs separate consent.
5. **Make withdrawal easy.** Tell people how to stop messages (for example, reply STOP on WhatsApp) and honour it.
6. **Keep it safe.** Turn on two-factor login for your builder and database. Run the **two-user safety test**: create two accounts and check neither can see the other's data.
7. **Don't keep it forever.** Decide how long you keep leads that don't become customers, and delete them after that.
8. **Have a contact person.** Publish an email (and ideally a name) for privacy questions and complaints, and reply promptly.
9. **Plan for a breach.** If data leaks, you must tell the affected people and the Data Protection Board of India.

## A consent line you can adapt

> I agree that [Business name] may use my name, WhatsApp number and answers to send my result and contact me about [topic]. I can withdraw anytime by replying STOP. [Privacy policy]

## Common mistakes

- **Pre-ticked consent boxes** or consent hidden in long terms.
- **Collecting data "just in case"**: every extra field is extra risk.
- **Sharing leads** with partners without telling people and getting consent.
- **Test data in public inboxes:** don't create test accounts with public email services. Use your own addresses.
- **No way to opt out** of WhatsApp messages.
